Here is the first hard part, finding redundancy without drowning in false matches. Two pages are a redundancy candidate when their normalized content overlaps at 0.75 or higher and they are about the same thing, and you decide the score with a method you write down and apply the same way to every pair, so a token or shingle overlap ratio, not a vibe. Record the actual score for every candidate pair so two runs can be checked against each other. Then cluster: group pages that are transitively similar above that threshold into one knowledge cluster, so if A matches B and B matches C, A B and C are one cluster even if A and C are a bit further apart. A cluster needs at least two members. Watch for the trap where two pages share a lot of boilerplate (the same header, the same template shell) but the actual body differs, that is not redundancy, so compare the meaningful body content and say so when you exclude a pair for that reason.

Second, schema drift across the databases that came from a shared template. Two databases are drifted when they hold the same logical fields but under different property names, so a "Due Date" in one and a "Deadline" in another that both carry the same kind of date for the same purpose. Match logical fields by their type, their sample values, and their role, not by name, and for each drifted pair record both property names, the database each lives in, and why you judged them the same field.
